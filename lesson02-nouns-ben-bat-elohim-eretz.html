What is a noun?

In first lesson we learned how to recognize names of God, people and places. These are called proper nouns. This lesson is also about nouns which are not names, these are called common nouns.

example, Genesis chapter 7 verses 6 and 7:

Noah was six hundred years old when the Flood came, waters upon the earth. Noah, with his sons, his wife and his sons' wives went into the ark because of the waters of the Flood.

Singular nouns בֵּןwords/w013_ben   בַּתwords/w082_bat   אֶרֶץwords/w021_eretz

Singular nouns don't have a particular form in Tanakh Hebrew (unlike German, say, where nouns always begin with a capital letter). In later lessons we will learn some ways to recognize nouns in the text.

All singular Tanakh nouns have at least 2 consonants. The most frequently occurring nouns have 2 or 3 consonants like בֵּןwords/w013_ben , בַּתwords/w082_bat and אֶרֶץwords/w021_eretz , but there are also many nouns with 4 consonants, for example, מִזְבֵּחַwords/w124_mizbeach altar and מָקוֺםwords/w125_makom place. There are also a few longer nouns, for example, מִשְׁפָּחָהwords/w154_mishpacha family, clan and מִלְחָמָהwords/w149_milchama war, battle.

Nouns often have a prefix, for example, הָאָרֶץ 1 words/w021_haaretz , the earth, and/or a suffix, for example, בְּנוֺ 3 words/w013_bno , his son. We will learn about these prefixes and suffixes in the next lessons. When a noun appears on its own, without any prefixes or suffixes, it's usually translated with "a" or "an". There is no Hebrew word for "a" or "an".

all nouns in BH have a gender, either masculine or feminine. With nouns referring to people, the gender is obvious - ish, man is masculaine and isha, woman, is feminine, but others are not obvious egeretz, land, is feminine and yom, day, is masculine. You don't need to learn these genders, but you need to be aware of them because they sometimes determine the form of verbs (and adjectives). Masculine nouns usually form their plurals with and feminine nouns with but some very frequently occurring nouns are exceptions: av/avot, isha/nashim, ir/arim, shana/shanim
