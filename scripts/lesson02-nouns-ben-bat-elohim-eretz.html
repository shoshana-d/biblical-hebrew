What is a noun?

In first lesson we learned how to recognize names of God, people and places. These are called proper nouns. This lesson is also about nouns which are not names, these are called common nouns.

example, Genesis chapter 7 verses 6 and 7:

Noah was six hundred years old when the Flood came, waters upon the earth. Noah, with his sons, his wife and his sons' wives went into the ark because of the waters of the Flood.

BH nouns don't have a particular form have a minimum of 2 consonants (am, av) but can have 3 (yad, yom, melech) , 2 or 3 mostly?

the 3 most frequently occurring common nouns plus bat

Common nouns, ben, elohim, eretz, singular/plural, (gender), no special form including construct, no Tanakh Hebrew word for of, when 2 nouns together, translated as though "of" between them but as we will see, need to be detective cos may have prefix and/or suffix need to learn to recognize the most frequently occurring -im, -ot endings always stressed, maqqef

table with singular and plural, Hebrew words have number, singular and plural like English, and also gender, even words like eretz. Just have to know about this, but don't really need to remember. Masculine nouns much more frequent than feminine nouns. Singular nouns don't have a particular form in Tanakh Hebrew (unlike German, say, where nouns always begin with a capital letter). In later lessons we will learn some ways to recognize nouns in the text.

all nouns in BH have a gender, either masculine or feminine. With nouns referring to people, the gender is obvious - ish, man is masculaine and isha, woman, is feminine, but others are not obvious egeretz, land, is feminine and yom, day, is masculine. You don't need to learn these genders, but you need to be aware of them because they sometimes determine the form of verbs (and adjectives). Masculine nouns usually form their plurals with and feminine nouns with but some very frequently occurring nouns are exceptions: av/avot, isha/nashim, ir/arim, shana/shanim

Some words that are singular in English are plural in Tanakh Hebrew elohim, panim
