The prefix vav וּמֹשֶׁהwords/_xx "and Moses"

There are 6 Hebrew "words": ו "and", הַ "the", ל "to" , בּ "in" , מִ "from" and כּ "like/as" , which never occur as separate words but are always attached to the beginning of another word. These "words" occur more than 100,000 times in the Tanakh, so it's really important to learn them.

In this lesson we learn about the most frequently occurring "word" ו  which is often translated as "and".

Words starting with ו

Hardly any words in the Tanakh actually have ו as their first letter.

Apart from these a word starting with vav is either the prefix "and" or a verb, about 50/50

Alter (this is about verbs I think) “[T]he characteristic biblical syntax is additive,” he writes. The simple “and” (the vav prefix), Alter writes, is “the way the ancient Hebrew writers saw the world, linked events in it,” yet previous translators have routinely elided the biblical “and”s. Thus, he notes, his translation of the four and a half verses in which Rebekah waters Abraham’s servant’s camels has 15 “and”s; the Revised English Bible has just five. It is impossible to miss the effect.

the prefix vav "and"

The consonant וְ alefbet_consonants_and_vowels/v is often added to the beginning of a word. When it's added to any word except a verb it indicates a connection, so it's often translated as "and". (And in fact, when it's added to a verb it's also often translated as "and".)

  alefbet_consonant_plus_vowel/vuh most frequent (about 50%) this is the "default" for "and" other vowels when particular combinations of consonant and vowel at the start of word, rules are complicated and you don't need to know them to recognoze vav.

וַ  alefbet_consonant_plus_vowel/va next most frequent, but mostly verbs

  alefbet_consonant_plus_vowel/u next most frequent, mostly not verbs

Sometimes וְ alefbet_consonants_and_vowels/v added at the beginning of a word becomes the vowel וּ alefbet_consonants_and_vowels/u When this happens, the וּ alefbet_consonants_and_vowels/u is always a separate syllable (ie, the next consonant in the word is the start of a new syllable.)

See וּ at the start of a word in Alefbet extra for more information.
